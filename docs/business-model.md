# TabTastic: proposed business model

**Find the block. Fix the flow !!**

TabTastic sells evidence that a specific customer journey works: try the task through accessibility information, show where it stops, review a source fix, and re-test the same task. Start with small fashion stores and restaurants, where an owner can understand the outcome in one demonstration: an item reaches the bag, or five dishes and prices can be read.

## Proposed pricing and distribution

| Plan | Proposed monthly price | Initial customer and purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Free check | $0 | Try one journey and see the recorded outcome. |
| Restaurant | $39 | Owners checking whether customers can find and read their menu. |
| Store | $49 | Small fashion merchants checking product selection and add to cart. |
| Agency | $299 | Agencies reviewing journeys and sharing evidence with their clients. |

These are proposed product prices, not verified willingness to pay, competitor averages, booked revenue, or evidence of paying customers. Usage allowances and production service commitments still need validation. The prototype uses Stripe **test mode** only.

The acquisition hypothesis is a free check that makes a concrete barrier visible. Conversion would come from owners who want reviewed repairs and repeat evidence after changes. The agency channel would let one partner bring multiple merchant relationships; no agency partnerships are claimed. Early validation should measure checks started, checks completed, evidence viewed, owner-approved repairs, paid conversion, and repeat use, without inventing targets or traction.

Retention would depend on useful dated evidence after a new product, menu change, theme update, or redesign. The current prototype runs **on demand** and repairs only its owned demo source. Continuous scheduled checks, alerts, and integrations for customer source code are roadmap work. Saved reports and replay tapes preserve previous observations; replay is not a fresh test.

## Illustrative Store unit economics

The following monthly budget for one $49 Store subscription is a planning assumption. None of these costs has been measured or verified in production, and they are not vendor quotes.

| Assumed cost per Store subscriber per month | Budget |
| --- | ---: |
| Hosted browser jobs | $5 |
| Model calls | $1 |
| Payment processing | $2 |
| Support allocation | $5 |
| **Assumed total** | **$13** |
| **Illustrative amount remaining** | **$36** |

That example leaves 73.5% of the subscription price after the assumed service costs. To make the sensitivity visible:

| Assumed total monthly service cost | Amount remaining from $49 | Illustrative margin |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $10 | $39 | 79.6% |
| $20 | $29 | 59.2% |
| $30 | $19 | 38.8% |

Margin here means `(price − assumed service cost) / price`. This is a cost sensitivity exercise, not a forecast or an observed gross margin. It excludes acquisition spending, engineering salaries, general overhead, taxes, refunds, and other costs. Browser duration, run frequency, model retries, and support demand could change the result materially. Paid plans need usage limits and measured costs before these economics can be relied on.

## Evidence and claim boundaries

The [source notes and screen citations](sources.md) contain the verified accessibility and market context, including each dataset's population, period, and limitations. They do not validate this pricing or cost model. The unverified claim about human testing costing $250 for three flows is excluded.

The claim is: **“The journeys we tested pass, with dated evidence.”** This model does not promise a site-wide accessibility outcome or prevention of legal action. There are no claimed customers, partnerships, or revenue in this prototype.
